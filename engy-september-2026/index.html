The 99-Cent Store with a Receipt

engy (Bittensor SN53)

Open-source AI models now beat the closed labs on price and are catching up on quality. What they have never had is a proper "shop": a bill, a guarantee you got what you paid for, and a place a business can point to. engy built that shop, priced it at roughly half what the frontier labs charge for their own weights, and since 12 August has even been buying back and burning its own token every time a customer pays.

01 · The Plain-English Version

Think Of Bittensor as a Machine That Builds Mini-Bitcoins

To understand how engy works, you just need to understand Bitcoin, and one twist on it.

Bitcoin's real invention was not the coin. It was the deal: anyone on Earth can plug in a computer and some electricity, do a pointless-looking task (guessing numbers), and get paid in a token that only has value if enough other people believe it will keep having value. Nobody has to apply. Nobody has to be trusted. You just show up with hardware and power, and you compete.

Bittensor takes that exact deal and generalises it. Instead of one contest (guess numbers, get bitcoin), it runs 128 separate contests side by side, each with its own task and its own token. One contest pays people to store files. One pays people to hunt for drug molecules. One pays people to train AI models. Each of these mini-economies is called a subnet, and each is, in effect, a mini-Bitcoin built around a specific job instead of an arbitrary one. engy is subnet 53, and its job is the one with the biggest, hungriest market of all: answering AI questions, cheaply, at scale.

Mark Jeffrey, a partner at Stillcore Capital, one of the largest funds inside Bittensor, put the origin story simply on a recent podcast. Open-weight models such as DeepSeek, Kimi and GLM had, by mid-2026, quietly become as good as the closed frontier labs' models and were eating their volume, but a raw open model is not a business, it's just a giant file anyone can download. Someone still has to turn it into a real product: billing, support, a guarantee it will actually be there when you need it. Jeffrey said as much publicly in July, naming five subnet contenders including engy.

"We're in the 99-cent store for world-class inference. That's a big deal, right?"Mark Jeffrey, Stillcore Capital, on the AI narrative for Bittensor podcast

engy is the subnet that took him up on it. Strip away every piece of Crypto vocabulary and it sells one thing: a developer sends a normal API request to api.engy.ai, gets an answer back, and is billed per token, no subscription, no minimum. Bittensor is the plumbing underneath, coordinating who does the work and paying them. The customer never has to see any of it. That invisibility is the entire point.

02 · Why It's So Cheap

The Same Trick That Sends Bitcoin Miners to Cheap Power

Stranded electricity, stranded GPUs, and one engineer's private trick for cramming a giant model onto small chips.

Electricity cannot easily be stored, so whenever a region produces more power than it can use, that power effectively goes to waste unless somebody local burns it immediately. That is the entire reason Bitcoin miners have always chased out-of-the-way places with cheap or "stranded" power, from Iran to rural China: the electricity was going spare anyway, so it could be had almost for free. Bittensor runs the same play, and engy has leaned on it hardest. Its operator, Hanlin AI, recruited gaming-grade GPUs and cheap power, much of it in China, rather than renting scarce, expensive datacentre chips.

The second piece is a genuine engineering trick. On 27 July 2026, Moonshot released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open model, the first in the three-trillion class, and the kind of thing that "should" need roughly a million dollars of specialised datacentre hardware to run properly. engy's founder, Ning Ren, had it serving full, uncompressed answers the same day, on 80 RTX 5090s: ordinary gaming cards anyone can buy for around $100,000 all-in, wired together over standard networking cable rather than exotic interconnects. Throughput was a modest 20 tokens a second on day one, untuned, but the same fleet took a smaller model, GLM-5.2, from 30 to 110 tokens a second in the following week through software improvements alone, a 3.7x gain with no new hardware at all. That pace of engineering, more than the chips themselves, is the scarce ingredient here.

"The most powerful open model on Earth, on the most abundant GPUs on Earth. Any lab, startup, or university can now own it, probe it, fine-tune it, run agents on it."Ning Ren (@totheagi), founder, Hanlin AI, 27 July 2026 · post passed 750,000 views

The upshot, in plain terms: engy prices its four original models at roughly half of what the labs charge for their own weights, and Kimi K3's output specifically undercuts a comparable closed frontier model by around 70%. Caching sweetens it further. Coding assistants and multi-step agents typically reuse 90% or more of their prompt on each call, and engy bills the repeated part at the cached rate automatically. At a 90% hit rate, the effective input cost on Kimi K3 falls to around $0.29 per million tokens, and switching an existing setup over is close to trivial: engy publishes ready-made configs for Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and Hermes, and in Claude Code's case the whole change is three lines in a settings file.

03 · The Receipt

The One Thing a Raw Open Model Can Never Prove: You Got It

Why "verified inference" is not a technical footnote, it is the actual product.

Imagine ordering the expensive steak and the kitchen quietly sending out a cheaper cut instead. Most nights you probably would not notice. It is still meat, it still arrives hot, and the easy parts of the meal taste the same either way. The problem shows up on the nights that matter: the tougher cut chews differently, and if you eat there every week for a year, the pattern becomes obvious, even if any single dinner does not prove anything on its own.

That is precisely the risk when a business buys access to an open-weight model like Kimi K3 through a third-party host. The host could be running the full, expensive version, or a quietly compressed copy that is cheaper for them to serve. In ordinary use you cannot tell: a compressed model does not fail loudly, it degrades at the margins, with slightly weaker reasoning and occasional small errors on the harder questions, which are usually exactly the questions a business paid the premium price to get right in the first place. Chain twenty of those small errors together inside an AI agent and you get one large failure, and the instinct is to blame your own prompt, not your provider, because nothing ever announced that a swap took place.

engy's fix is to make that swap provable rather than just promised. Before a single request is served, each model's exact weights are published as a cryptographic fingerprint, a fixed reference anyone can check against. The operator then signs a commitment on every request confirming which model answered, and auditors challenge a random sample of those commitments after the fact. If the arithmetic does not check out, the operator is caught; nothing about it depends on trusting the hardware or the operator's word.

"A failed opening is proof of cheating, not bad luck. No TEEs, no trusted hardware. The proof pins the math, not the machine."engy protocol README, github.com/hanlinai/engy

Put simply: it is the difference between a hallmark stamped into a gold bar and a shopkeeper's word that it's real. One you can check for yourself; the other you just have to believe. engy is not the only project selling that stamp on Bittensor (Beat 05 covers the closest rival, which is technically further along), but it is currently the only one attaching it to a frontier-scale model at no extra charge, rather than as a costly add-on.

04 · Growing Pains, Getting Fixed

From General-Admission Complaints to a Proper Door Policy

Two changes on 11 August answered the two loudest criticisms of engy at once.

Through the Summer, engy took real criticism from two directions. Some miners complained that access to the lucrative frontier-model work was gated behind a whitelist they could not get onto, an odd look for a network built to be permissionless. Separately, sceptics questioned whether any of the money changing hands ever found its way back to token holders, rather than just funding the team's own infrastructure. Both complaints were fair, and on 11 August engy answered them together.

The fix works like a venue that used to only let people in off a promoter's list and now runs two doors instead. The first is general admission: anyone with qualifying enterprise-grade hardware, specific configurations of H200s, B300s or H100s depending on the model, can now mine frontier workloads permissionlessly, no application needed. The second is a proper VIP contract room: a tightened enterprise whitelist, now reserved for applicants with a registered company, the ability to sign a formal agreement covering zero data retention and service levels, and at least $1 million of stable GPU capacity of their own. The old, informal whitelist closed at the end of the epoch it was announced in.

The other half of the 11 August update, a usage-funded buyback-and-burn programme, is significant enough to sit at the centre of the investment case, so Beat 08 covers it on its own. What is worth noting here is simply that both fixes were reactive: engy shipped them in direct response to public criticism, which is a reasonable way to build trust, though it also means the criticism was fair to begin with.

The reliability numbers back up genuine progress. Gateway uptime over the trailing 90 days has risen from 93.55% a month ago to 99.65% today, and Kimi K3 specifically, the model under the most strain, has gone from 90.86% to 99.87%. An enterprise contract is typically written at 99.9%, so engy is not there yet, but it has closed most of the gap in under a month.

The honest caveat: The TAO Daily, the sector's trade press, reported one user who paid in TAO for engy credits, did not receive them, and waited roughly nine to ten hours without a response. Response times were flagged separately as an area needing work. A company selling infrastructure to enterprises eventually needs a real support desk, and right now the only published point of contact is a founder's email address. That is the more mundane, and arguably more important, risk than anything about the technology. But let's be fair: this is a small, young startup, so this delay is pretty much to be accepted, and one that can and will be fixed by a team that has been ambitious and responsive to criticism so far.

05 · The Competition

Five Rivals Sell Proof or Cheap Compute. Nobody Else Sells Both.

The closest technical competitor is ahead on engineering. It is behind on everything a customer actually buys.

AI compute on Bittensor is not one business, it is a stack of them. Some subnets simply rent out raw hardware. Others take that hardware and serve finished answers without ever proving which model produced them. engy sits at the narrow top of that stack: not the compute, not even the answer, but a receipt proving which model produced it.

Verathos is the one worth taking seriously. It proves things faster (the check happens inside the serving software itself, in single-digit percentage overhead, rather than after the fact), it is fully open-source under MIT, it already runs permissionless mining where engy has only just opened enrolment, and it has verified training built and waiting to switch on. Where it falls short is scale: it has nothing comparable to a 2.8-trillion-parameter frontier model, no coding-focused offering, and its cheapest tier runs a previous-generation model at roughly eleven times engy's price on a like-for-like verified basis. Verathos is also barely used yet, with four GitHub stars and three forks to its name.

engy is not the better proof system. It is the only one currently attached to a model worth paying for, at a price that does not charge extra for the proof. That is a lead built on engineering speed rather than a structural moat, and speed like that compresses over time.

06 · The Team

A Second-Time Founder, a Self-Mining Row, and a Trip To Stanford

What we know, what is credibly rumoured, and where the two get blurred on Crypto Twitter.

Hanlin AI, the team behind engy, describes itself as engineers from frontier AI labs working on model post-training and inference. Founder Ning Ren, previously at Google Brain working on TensorFlow and TPUs with a computer science background from Fudan, is not a first-time Bittensor operator: Hanlin AI also runs TrajectoryRL (subnet 11), which post-trains small open models into specialised agents. That matters more than the CV line, because it means the team has already taken a Bittensor incentive mechanism through a full cycle, designing the scoring, defending it against gaming, and keeping operators engaged, which is precisely where technically strong teams most often fail on this network. No external investors or funding rounds have been announced; subnet 53 itself previously belonged to an unrelated trading-strategy business before Hanlin AI took it over.

The team has also drawn genuine controversy over self-mining, meaning Hanlin AI's own operators fill some of the requests that would otherwise go to independent miners. Critics call this a conflict of interest on a network meant to be open; defenders point out engy has been transparent about it from the outset, more so than several subnets that self-mine quietly, and that the practice mainly reflects a hardware bar (Kimi K3 specifically needs the newer B300 chips, which most independent miners do not yet own) rather than favouritism. Whichever reading is right, it is a live reputational risk worth watching rather than a settled matter.

On 31 August, Ning posted a photograph from Stanford's Hoover Tower with no caption beyond the time of day. On its own that is nothing. Read alongside the team's separately reported hunt for US and EU-based compute they can show to enterprise customers, and roughly $5 million of the team's own capital said to be committed toward securing B300 GPU capacity specifically for US clients, it is at least a plausible signal of a push into US enterprise sales and hiring. None of this is confirmed by the team, and the more mundane explanation, that a young founder took a trip, is equally possible. We are flagging it as a data point to watch for a named site or customer, not as news.

07 · The Risks

Four Ways This Goes Wrong

None of these are specific to engy alone, but engy is exposed to all four at once.
1

Bittensor keeps changing its own rules

The network has changed net token flow, staking rewards, the deregistration process and conviction-locking rules inside a single year, generally after teams had already committed capital under the old rules. Subnets can be pruned from the network entirely, and the mechanism that decides who gets pruned weighs token price over whether the underlying business actually works. Mark Creaser, CEO of DSV Fund, has been blunt about this risk: "a casino at least keeps the same rules all night. Bittensor changes the table mid-hand and calls it an upgrade." engy's rapid re-rating puts it far from that queue today; a sustained drawdown would not.

2

The proof lane is contested, and engy is not the technical leader

Verathos ships a lower-overhead proof with no round trip, a fully open protocol and live permissionless mining today. engy's edge is that it can serve a genuinely frontier-scale model and Verathos, for now, cannot. If Verathos or another rival reaches frontier scale before engy closes its own enterprise-grade reliability and support gaps, the ordering of this category changes.

3

Enterprises may simply not switch

The broader bear case for the whole open-model trade: enterprise spend on open models actually fell from 19% to 11% of total AI budget over the past year, according to a16z's own CIO survey, because switching costs are real once agents, context stores and integrations are already built around a proprietary API. Cheaper tokens do not automatically win the highest-value work, however good the receipt attached to them.

4

Model provenance, key-person risk, and a support desk that isn't one yet

All seven models engy serves are Chinese open weights, which brings both compliance questions for regulated customers and the live regulatory risk that a future US administration restricts them; a credible Western open model on the menu would meaningfully de-risk this. Public activity also runs through essentially one founder and one visible contributor. And, more mundanely, a customer who paid in TAO for credits reportedly went nine to ten hours without receiving them or a response. Cheap, verified tokens are not yet the same thing as a business enterprises can safely depend on.

08 · The Money

Why It Fits the Dragonfly Thesis

Our standing checklist for whether a token is investable: growing users, network effects, real revenue, a durable competitive edge, and a live mechanism that returns value to holders. engy is the first Bittensor subnet we have seen clear all of it.

Every token we look at gets weighed against the same questions before we call it investable: is the user base growing; does each new user make the network more valuable to the ones already there; is there real, sustainable revenue; is there a definable competitive advantage that lets the protocol hold its edge against rivals (Beat 05 covers engy's case); and, critically, is there a live or imminent mechanism that returns that revenue to holders, rather than just to the team. Below, we set out how engy measures up against that framework.

Usage. One subnet tracker measured epoch 11 already 72% ahead of the whole of epoch 10 after just 82% of the time had elapsed, a daily run-rate roughly double the prior epoch's. engy's own status page independently shows daily volumes into the tens of millions of tokens, and Kimi K3's requests are getting fewer but heavier, longer and more successful, a pattern that looks more like real production workloads settling in than one-off testing.

Network effects. Every agent framework that adds a three-line engy config, every new miner who joins through the new permissionless lane, and every enterprise that signs the new whitelist agreement makes the gateway more liquid and more credible for the next one. The first named integration, a Robinhood Chain trading-analysis terminal called Teresa, reported by The TAO Daily on 3 August, used engy specifically to remove the cost and privacy limits that had been rationing how often it could run its own multi-model debates, an early but real example of the flywheel working as intended.

Revenue. This is where a subnet differs from a normal company: engy discloses no revenue figures directly, but the buyback data effectively is the revenue figure, because engy can only burn what customers actually paid. $28,144 has been burned across 13 onchain transactions between 12 August and 2 September, verified directly from engy's own buyback page rather than taken from social media. Separately, working back from published emission data, Hanlin AI's 18% owner cut of subnet issuance was worth in the region of $8,000 a day, or roughly $2.9 million annualised, at the token price in early August; that figure is Dragonfly's own back-of-envelope calculation from public data, not a disclosed number, and it is worth noting it would exist whether or not engy had a single paying customer.

Which means the team was funded whether or not the product sold. Now, for the first time, we can also see that customers are actually paying, in dollars anyone can verify onchain.

The mechanism. Before 12 August, there was no link at all between engy's revenue and its token; money coming in just paid for infrastructure. The new programme buys back and burns engy's token using real usage revenue rather than a fixed emissions schedule, so more customer spend mechanically means more buying pressure and less token supply. It is a genuine fee switch, and engy was explicit that it exists specifically to answer the "does revenue reach holders" criticism. The honest caveat is scale: $28,144 of lifetime burns is a rounding error against a roughly $36 million network value, and against the token's own daily new issuance, which one analyst estimated at around $40,000-plus a day in gross terms before buybacks. It is a real mechanism, but still a thin one, worth re-checking monthly rather than treating it as proven.

engy, in our view, is the clearest example yet of an open-model business earning the receipt it sells: real price advantage, a working proof of which model actually answered, and now a mechanism that puts customer revenue behind the token rather than just the team. None of that makes it a finished company - the support desk, the shifting Bittensor rulebook, and a faster-moving rival on the proof side are all still live. What earns the position is the direction of travel: every criticism levelled at engy this Summer was answered within weeks, in public, in numbers we could verify ourselves. We are initiating a position sized to those risks, and we will keep checking the same things every month - usage, the moat, revenue, and the buyback - before deciding whether to add.
