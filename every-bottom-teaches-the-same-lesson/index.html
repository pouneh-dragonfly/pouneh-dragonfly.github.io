Every Bottom Teaches the Same Lesson. Nobody Learns It.

Every cycle, investors convince themselves the risk is greater this time. It never is: the reasons are always the same very human ones, and so is the opportunity hiding underneath them.

Pouneh Bligaard, Founder & CIO·26 August 2026·10 min read

Source: Bitcoin Obituaries (bitcoindeaths.com), tracking the number of times Bitcoin has been declared dead — 472 times at the last count.

One year ago, Bitcoin started falling and was soon declared finished. Again. And Altcoins – those newer, riskier ‘brothers’ of Bitcoin? Even worse – ‘what idiot even thought these new-fangled networks were worth anything?’ Not much fun when you are trying to convince people they should allocate to Crypto in their portfolios…

This report is about the pattern underneath that verdict: the one that repeats every cycle, that nobody quite learns, and the businesses that were quietly being built while everyone else relearned, the hard way, that fear is a terrible driver of investing.

Part One · The Damning Verdict

Everybody Knew Bitcoin Was Finished

Start with the cycle still fresh enough to sting, because it is the clearest recent proof of a pattern we keep coming back to.

The Fall

From its all-time high of $126,000 in October 2025, Bitcoin fell over 50% to the low $60,000s. The autopsies wrote themselves: stablecoins were eating its use case in payments, quantum computing was going to break it wide open, ETFs would sell every single BTC, it was a relic with no price floor. The Crypto Fear and Greed Index hit the lowest reading ever recorded.

The wider sector numbers were miles worse. Total Crypto market capitalisation fell from around $3.18 trillion in January to $2.11 trillion by June, more than a trillion dollars erased in six months. Ethereum dropped over 40%, and Solana, XRP and most other major tokens fell by similar margins. Activity thinned alongside the prices: total value locked across decentralised finance contracted from $114.5 billion to $69.4 billion.

The Autopsy

Crazy as it sounds, none of it began in Crypto. A hawkish Federal Reserve, an oil shock out of the Middle East, and especially a powerful rotation of capital into the AI buildout, itself approaching $700 billion in spending for the year, made Crypto a convenient source of funds for the dominant narrative of the period. Stablecoins were said to have already won the one payments use case Bitcoin ever had. Quantum computing was coming for its cryptography, and the community had no clean, one-line answer for the wallets it might expose. None of it was baseless. All of it was old news – the perennial ‘what about’ reasons that pop up in every downturn – dressed up as a new reason to give up at the bottom. The strange part this time is that fundamentals were improving rapidly throughout. Rational investors know fundamentals are what matter most, and that price tells you the least exactly when fear runs highest.

Part Two · The Same Signal

Sell Signals You Can Set a Watch To

The Inverse Indicator

Then, right on schedule, the signal arrived. On 3 August, Jim Cramer, having held Bitcoin through years of cycles, announced he was selling it all, citing quantum risk. Crypto Twitter would normally celebrate such an “Inverse Cramer” call and excitedly declare it the bottom. But fear was running too high for that – people refused to believe the most reliable contrary indicator the market has ever seen!

It was the sign investors should have heeded. Within three weeks Bitcoin surged 25% in a single week, and broke back above $80,000 for the first time in over three months.

That is the pattern, every cycle. Lower prices aren’t usually about a change in the fundamentals. They change the story people tell themselves about the fundamentals.

Part Three · Business Was Booming

What Was Actually Happening Underneath

Here is what actually changed while everyone was busy writing Bitcoin's obituary. Crypto built a real revenue engine.

From Nice Idea to Real Revenue

The industry will generate close to $20 billion in onchain fees this year, nearly matching the 2021 peak, but for entirely different reasons. In 2021, Ethereum alone took over 40% of those fees from users overpaying to mint the latest NFT during a speculative frenzy. Today, DeFi and finance applications generate 63% of all onchain fees, from trading, lending and derivatives that people use because they are useful, not because there was no alternative. Transaction costs have fallen roughly 90% in the same period.

Paying Shareholders, Not Renting Users

Seventy one protocols now generate over $100 million a year in revenue, thirty two of them got there within a single year. Value distributed to token holders hit an all-time high of $1.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, up 50% on late 2021, even as speculative token incentive spending collapsed from $2.8 billion to under $100 million. That is real yield replacing subsidies.

The Regulatory Unlock

None of that happened by accident. The GENIUS Act in the US and MiCA in Europe gave nearly 1,100 protocols the regulatory clarity to run token buybacks and burns that would have got a lawyer fired three years ago. DeFi applications now trade at a median 17 times fees, in growth software territory, not lottery ticket territory. Onchain fees are projected to reach $32 billion in 2026, up 63%, and every bit of that growth is expected to come from customers paying for apps, not speculation.

The Re-Rating

The multiple gap either side of that 17 times median is the tell. It is the premium the market still pays for infrastructure with network effects and security guarantees applications cannot replicate, set against how cheap the applications themselves remain for the revenue they now generate. We at Dragonfly have written about this opportunity, calling the successful apps of this cycle the “cash machines” of the future – the same role apps came to play in the earlier build-out of the internet.

Part Four · The Proof

We Have Always Held that Fundamentals Matter Most. Then They Did.

Down Less, For the Right Reasons

At Dragonfly we are seasoned enough as investors to know that prices in very early-stage tech sectors like Crypto often move violently around fair value. There is a double whammy at play: prices can temporarily ignore attractive, improving fundamentals as investors become transfixed by every perceived risk out there, and the elastic band can stretch a long way from what is actually happening on the ground, however well the underlying businesses are doing. But the snap back is then particularly sharp, because fair value has also been moving up dramatically through the downturn. It is heartening that the market is starting to recognise that this once obscure, misunderstood, under-the-radar sector is now producing spectacular business success – fast-growing revenues and powerful network effects, the dream of every long-term investor.

‘The $20 Billion Shift: Why Crypto’s Revenue Story Has Changed’, Forbes Digital Assets, November 2025.

The Names That Rose Anyway

Proof of principle came during what was, by market backdrop, the hardest period in the Fund's history. Three Fund holdings led the way. Hyperliquid generated $320 million of revenue and $1.34 trillion of trading volume over the half, burning more than 41 million tokens along the way. Venice built a consumer app with more than 4 million users and has been profitable since its first quarter, buying back and burning its own token with every dollar of revenue. Morpho became the embedded lending layer behind Robinhood Earn and Coinbase, processing over $1.9 billion in loans and pushing its total value locked past $7 billion. Each rose for the same reason: real, fast-rising revenue, transparently accruing to the token holder, at exactly the moment the wider market decided fundamentals no longer mattered.

The Smart Money Starts Buying Crypto Tokens

We have long held that investing in Crypto tokens will become indistinguishable from investing in early-stage equity. Institutions already seem to be reaching the same conclusion, with rather more capital behind them. In February, Apollo, which manages $940 billion, agreed to buy as much as 9% of Morpho. BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, bought Uniswap's tokens while listing its own tokenised Treasury fund on that same protocol. Crypto dealmaking hit close to $94 billion in the first half of 2026, roughly 26 times the year before.

It is worth sitting with the irony that some of the clearest cash flows anywhere in technology today are in Crypto, not in an artificial intelligence sector where nearly everyone is still subsidising everyone else.

Part Five · The Opportunity

The Market Is Still Fearful and Disbelieving

Institutions Are Building Onchain and Buying Infrastructure Furiously

Layer the institutional picture on top. Roughly $75 billion flowed into Bitcoin and Ether ETFs in 2025 alone. BlackRock's CFO has said all of their ETFs will be tokenised within the year. Stripe has acquired Bridge to build stablecoin payment infrastructure. And of the roughly 700 million people who own Crypto today, only about 10% actively use an onchain application. That gap is the growth still sitting on the table.

630 million Crypto owners who have never touched an onchain app

~70 million active onchain users out of ~700 million people who own Crypto. The rest are not sceptics who need convincing; they are already customers of the asset class, waiting for a reason to become customers of what is built on top of it. Source: Andreessen Horowitz, via 1kx onchain revenue report.

The Hundred Trillion Dollar Question

The more powerful force is structural rather than cyclical. Every major financial institution is now building on stablecoins and reorganising around tokenisation, and the sums under discussion are measured in the tens of trillions of dollars, with some estimates running as high as $100 trillion moving onchain within a handful of years. Let's not forget that the whole altcoin market – where all this onchain building is taking place – is valued at a paltry $1 trillion on a good day! Assets moving onchain are only the first-order effect. What compounds from there is transaction velocity: a market that trades every hour of every day, populated increasingly by autonomous agents transacting at machine speed, does not generate twice the fee revenue of the market it replaces. History suggests it generates orders of magnitude more. We do not send twice as many emails as we once sent letters; we send a thousand times as many.

The Catalyst on the Calendar

Nearer term, the CLARITY Act, the most significant piece of US digital-asset legislation to date, cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May on a bipartisan vote. Its passage would be the starting gun for a set of applications still priced at only a few billion dollars apiece despite real and growing revenue. Its chances of passing may look slim, but that may matter less than it seems: the pace of TradFi's onchain build-out means that even a less favourable future administration would find this adoption politically difficult to reverse. Uber and Airbnb offer the precedent – both grew into giants without the benefit of regulatory clarity as a tailwind, and neither could later be dislodged.

In one sense, everything we hoped for in this industry came true over the past twelve months, while the price fell. Real usage. Real revenue. Real regulatory clarity. Real institutional adoption. A rational investor looks at a 50% drawdown against that backdrop and sees an opportunity. Human nature looks at the same price chart and searches for a reason to doubt the thesis, anything that would make the lower price feel justified.

We are human too. It has been painful living through Crypto's deep sector drawdowns, but revisiting the strength of the traction enjoyed by this young sector's winners only reinforced our conviction.
