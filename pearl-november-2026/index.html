The Gold in the Copper Mine

Pearl (PEARL): a Bitcoin for the age of AI

Pearl wants the graphics chips that already answer the world's AI questions to mint a scarce digital asset as a by-product, the way a copper mine produces gold. If it works, Pearl becomes the second great proof-of-work network, secured by the largest build-out of computing power in history. Today it is seven months old, mined mostly for the gold alone, and priced at 0.06% of Bitcoin.

Written by Pouneh Bligaard · 20 November 2026
For professional investors only · Sources listed under Methodology
Executive summary

Why the World's Biggest Copper Mines Also Dig for Gold

Mining investors have a phrase for it: by-product credits. A large copper mine pulls gold and silver from the same ore. Because the rock is being blasted, hauled and crushed anyway, the extra metal costs very little to recover, and its sale is deducted from the cost of producing the copper. At a few exceptional mines, the gold has at times paid for most of the digging.

Pearl applies that logic to artificial intelligence. Every time an AI model answers a question, a graphics chip multiplies huge tables of numbers together, a calculation known as matrix multiplication. Pearl is a new blockchain, built from Bitcoin's own code, in which that same calculation is also the work that secures the network and creates new coins. An AI data centre serving paying customers is the copper mine. PEARL is the gold that comes out of the same rock.

The catch, and it is a large one: today almost nobody is running the copper mine. Most of Pearl's miners crush rock purely for the gold, multiplying random numbers to win coins and selling them on arrival. Whether Pearl becomes a durable store of value depends on reversing that, and the first real test went live on Pearl's trial network this week.

Investment thesis

Pearl is a high-risk, high-optionality bet on a third Crypto money, after Bitcoin and Zcash: a store of value whose security budget is paid for by the AI boom rather than competing with it. Reaching even 1% of Bitcoin's value would mean roughly nine times today's price, after allowing for a year of new supply. Against that sits a protocol whose central promise of useful work is not yet delivered, heavy daily issuance that miners sell almost in full, and no revenue returned to holders. Asymmetric, unproven and only suitable at small size.

01 · The problem

Bitcoin's Security Bill Is Coming Due

The world's leading store of value is losing its miners to the AI boom.

Bitcoin is protected by miners who spend real money on machines and electricity to add each new block of transactions. They are paid in newly created bitcoin plus transaction fees. Every four years the new-coin reward halves, while the cost of the machines and the power does not. Over the long run, Bitcoin's security therefore depends on one of two things: transaction fees rising sharply, or the bitcoin price roughly doubling with every halving. Neither is guaranteed.

The miners have already done the arithmetic. AI hosting earns far more per megawatt than mining bitcoin, and the listed miners are moving their power contracts, and their balance sheets, accordingly.

A second concern sits alongside the first. Quantum computers may one day break the cryptography that protects older bitcoin wallets, and Bitcoin's developer culture is famously slow to change. Neither problem is fatal today. Together, they have opened the first serious debate in a decade about whether Bitcoin's dominance as digital gold is permanent.

For years, investors assumed there could only ever be one Crypto money. Zcash has broken that assumption, and is now worth around $14 billion on a single idea: private money. If a second store of value can win on privacy, a third may be able to win on the durability of its security. That is the gap Pearl is aiming at.

"Pearl does not need to replace Bitcoin for the investment to work."Ryan Watkins, co-founder, Syncracy Capital

02 · The solution

Bitcoin With a Day Job

Same rules, same scarcity. A different kind of work.

Pearl's main network went live on 27 April 2026. To see what is new about it, it helps to start with how Bitcoin keeps itself honest.

Mining is a lottery you cannot buy your way into. Bitcoin has no bank or company keeping its ledger. Instead, thousands of independent "miners" compete to write the next page of transactions, called a block. Tickets for this lottery cannot be bought with money, only earned with computing effort: each attempt at a puzzle is one ticket, almost all of them lose, and roughly every ten minutes one wins. The winner writes the next page and is paid in newly created coins. Because winning takes enormous amounts of real electricity and hardware, an attacker trying to rewrite history would have to outspend everyone else combined. That cost is what makes the ledger secure.

The puzzle itself is deliberately pointless. Bitcoin miners push numbers through a scrambling formula called SHA-256, trillions of times a second, hunting for a result that happens to begin with enough zeros. Think of trying every combination on a padlock: slow to find, instant for anyone to check, and worth nothing once it springs open. Bitcoin spends a vast amount of electricity producing lottery tickets and nothing else.

Pearl keeps the lottery and changes the puzzle. It is built from Bitcoin's own software, with an identical fixed supply and open competition, but its miners earn tickets by doing matrix multiplication, the arithmetic every AI model runs on.

The term sounds forbidding but describes ordinary sums at enormous scale. Picture a restaurant chain with one spreadsheet recording how many of each dish every branch sold, and a second listing what goes into each recipe. Multiplying the two tables, row by column, tells you how much flour, oil and salt each branch used. An AI model does exactly this with tables holding billions of numbers: your question is turned into numbers, multiplied through the model's tables layer upon layer, and the result is translated back into words. Almost all the electricity an AI data centre consumes goes on this one operation.

The hard part was always checking. A padlock combination is instant to verify. A multiplication is not: if a stranger claims to have multiplied two giant tables for you, the obvious way to check is to do the sum yourself, which doubles the cost and defeats the purpose. Most decentralised AI networks, including Bittensor, live with that inefficiency, relying on validators who re-test miners' work and grade answers whose quality is partly a matter of opinion.

Pearl's answer comes from a 2025 paper by Ilan Komargodski, Itamar Schen and Omri Weinstein, now Pearl's co-founder and chief executive. In effect, it makes the multiplication print its own tamper-proof receipt as it goes. While the chip works, a sliver of extra arithmetic mixes a fingerprint of the calculation into a lottery ticket. The ticket cannot be produced without genuinely doing the work, yet checking a winning one takes a tiny fraction of the effort. That single trick turns the most common calculation in AI into a Bitcoin-style puzzle.

"It's Bitcoin, but instead of SHA-256, it's matrix multiplication. And that's it."Tulip King, Crypto investor, livestream interview

The design is deliberately spare. There is no smart-contract platform, no validator committee and no judgement about whether the AI work was worthwhile; the network checks only the receipt. The bet is economic rather than technical: a data centre already doing that multiplication for a paying customer should rationally collect the lottery tickets as well. Simplicity matters here. The networks that tried to beat Bitcoin with extra features mostly lost; Pearl's pitch is Bitcoin's own rules with a more useful engine.

Who is behind it. Pearl is built by Pearl Research Labs in Tel Aviv. Weinstein is a complexity theorist with a Princeton doctorate who has worked in Columbia's theoretical computer science group, holds a post at the Hebrew University and lists industry experience at Nvidia and Vast Data. Israeli business daily TheMarker reported in October that the company had raised about $55 million of equity plus a $75 million vehicle to build GPU capacity, from investors including Lightspeed, Ribbit Capital and Square Peg. Pearl has not confirmed the round on its own channels, a point we return to under Risks.

03 · How it works

One Shift, Two Pay Packets

Why an AI provider would mine, and why most do not yet.

Picture a company renting out Nvidia chips to answer AI questions. The customer's payment already covers the hardware and the power. If the same multiplications also generate PEARL lottery tickets, any coins won are close to pure profit, the by-product credit of our opening. Pearl's supporters call this duplex mining: one piece of work, two sources of income.

The economics are designed to spread. AI answering is fast becoming a price war with razor-thin margins, so a provider earning PEARL on the side can pass part of it to customers as a discount. Rivals then face a choice: mine too, or lose on price. Each new provider adds mining power, strengthening the network's security and the coin's credibility as a store of value, which in turn raises the value of the subsidy.

The first proof points exist. In May, Together AI, a leading AI cloud provider, launched a version of Google's Gemma model priced more than 25% below its usual rate, with the discount funded by the expected value of PEARL rewards. This week Pearl Research Labs opened its own FP8 endpoint on OpenRouter, the largest marketplace for AI models; one prominent investor reports it was already handling around four times the token volume of NEAR AI within two days, a social-media figure we have not verified.

"In some ways, you could think about NVIDIA as an ASIC for Pearl mining."Milk Road Crypto, YouTube interview with Evan Van Ness

An ASIC is a chip built for a single job; Bitcoin is mined on them, and they are worthless for anything else, so Bitcoin's security competes with the AI boom for power. Pearl is mined on the same Nvidia hardware the AI industry is buying by the million, so its potential security budget grows with AI investment rather than against it. One estimate cited by its supporters is that capturing just 1% of AI compute would give Pearl many times Bitcoin's raw mining power.

Gold without the copper

Here is the honest problem. Nothing obliges a miner to do useful work. The network checks the receipt, not what was being multiplied, so tables of random numbers produce tickets just as valid as a customer's AI question, and faster, because a dedicated miner never pauses to serve anyone. A June research preprint measured a network of roughly 320,000 graphics-card equivalents drawing an estimated 112 megawatts and found the dominant mining software contained no AI code at all. Blockworks estimated earlier this month that fewer than 1% of blocks matched a real AI model.

Part of the reason is a mismatch of units. Computers can store numbers at different levels of precision, much as a recipe can measure in grams or in rough spoonfuls. Most AI servers today work in a compact format called FP8. Pearl's original mining accepted only a different format, INT7, which almost no AI provider uses. A provider wanting to mine had to convert its model, like a factory retooling a metric production line to imperial for a single customer, and then run it more slowly.

The FP8 upgrade, live on Pearl's test network since 17 November, switches mining to the format the industry already speaks: FP8 accounted for 58% of the OpenRouter endpoints that disclosed one in October. It has a side effect. In FP8, different chip families round the final digit slightly differently, the way two calculators can disagree in the eighth decimal place, and a blockchain cannot tolerate that because every checker must reach exactly the same answer. So Pearl standardises the calculator, limiting mining to Nvidia's two newest data-centre families, Hopper and Blackwell. That will shut out about two-thirds of today's mining power, most of it older and gaming hardware.

Pearl's team says the slowdown for a server mining while serving customers should fall to between 1% and 5%. The first independent test found far more.

Kairos found one further limit. Answering an AI question involves two jobs. First the model reads the whole prompt in one go, a single large multiplication, like a printing press running a full sheet. Then it writes the reply one word at a time, each a small multiplication of its own, more like typing a letter by hand. In the build Kairos tested, mining works only during the reading stage; forcing it into the writing stage wrecked the answers, with accuracy on a maths test falling to about 1%. In copper-mine terms, the gold comes out during only part of the shift.

Kairos called the upgrade real progress and expects the numbers to move as the software matures. For now, the cleanest economics for a GPU owner remain dedicated mining, and the useful-work thesis remains a work in progress.

By the measures that matter for a proof-of-work network, adoption has been rapid. Mining power rose from about 24 to roughly 47 exahashes a second between June and July, and was estimated in a 45 to 70 range in October. Kairos's census counted about 24,200 payout addresses across six mining pools, covering 91% of the network. Since the Coinbase and Kraken listings, weekly exchange deposits have more than doubled to 29.4 million PEARL, spot trading runs at around $23-25m a day across seven venues, and a perpetual future trades on Hyperliquid.

The market value has followed, with violent swings along the way. From a first peak in late May, the price fell by around 85% into the summer as the earliest miners sold, then rose roughly ninefold to a new high in October. Today PEARL is the 77th-largest Crypto asset by market value.

Pearl's advantages are real but young. First, it is the only proof-of-useful-work network running at scale, with tier-one exchange listings and a peer-reviewed cryptographic foundation, where earlier attempts never left the whitepaper. Second, its hardware is the most valuable compute on earth. Every Nvidia chip bought for AI is a potential Pearl miner, so the network's ceiling rises with each hyperscaler budget. Third, it enjoys what Syncracy calls a last-mover advantage: launching into a world that already has wallets, custodians, exchanges and modern cryptography, it shipped post-quantum signatures from day one and plans native privacy for 2027, problems Bitcoin has yet to solve.

How durable are these moats? Less than they look. The code is open source, so a rival can copy the mining design. What cannot be copied quickly is the accumulated mining power, exchange liquidity and track record of surviving crashes, the qualities that took Bitcoin a decade to earn. Pearl is seven months into that process. Its real moat, if it comes, will be being first to lock in AI providers' habits: once data centres routinely mine PEARL alongside paid work, a copycat would need to pry them away.

06 · Tokenomics

A Hard Cap With a Wide-Open Tap

Scarce in the long run, heavily diluting in the short run.

PEARL has a fixed maximum supply of 2.1 billion coins, 100 times Bitcoin's 21 million. Rather than Bitcoin's four-yearly halvings, new issuance declines smoothly with every block, with about half the total supply scheduled to exist roughly four years after launch. Blocks arrive about every three minutes and 14 seconds, each currently paying around 2,150 new coins, which works out at roughly 960,000 PEARL a day, close to $2 million at today's price.

That is the single most important number for an investor. On our estimate, circulating supply will grow by about 75% over the next twelve months, and more than double within two years. Miners sell almost everything they earn: Kairos found that 88% to 93% of mining payouts reached an exchange or over-the-counter desk, implying around $56 million a month of steady selling. For the price merely to stand still, new buyers must absorb that flow.

The fair-launch question. Pearl sold no coins to investors and reserved none for its team. But mining difficulty started at its minimum, so the first 40,000 blocks arrived at roughly 13-second intervals instead of three minutes, and 121.7 million coins, about a third of today's supply, were mined in the first six days. Blockworks notes the whitepaper describes the low starting difficulty as partly intended to fund the team behind the project, and Pearl Research Labs has never disclosed its holdings. One widely followed analyst estimates the company, team and shareholders hold around 98 million coins, roughly a quarter of the circulating supply, with a commitment, unenforceable onchain, to lock at least half until May 2027.

What holders receive. Nothing, directly. PEARL has no fee switch, no buyback and no staking yield. Its value rests entirely on two sources of demand: investors who want a scarce store of value, and AI providers who need the coins' expected worth to fund their discounts. That makes PEARL a money-like asset in the mould of Bitcoin and Zcash, and the investment case a bet on monetary premium, not on cash flows.

"Their equity holders make money even if $PEARL holders don't."Ignas, DeFi analyst, on the $130m equity raise

07 · Growth drivers and catalysts

The Tide Behind the Trade

The AI build-out is the largest capital programme of our time. Pearl is designed to ride it.

The four largest hyperscalers are on course to spend roughly $725 to $750 billion on capital expenditure this year, much of it on Nvidia chips. Pearl does not need a meaningful share of that hardware to matter; it needs a small, rising proportion of operators to decide that mining alongside paid work is free money. Syncracy's thesis anticipates a "P-Day", when Pearl's mining footprint overtakes Bitcoin's in compute and energy, possibly before the end of the decade. Between here and there, we see six specific catalysts.

1

FP8 on mainnet

Estimated by Syncracy for around January 2027; no date set and code not yet merged. The single biggest test of the thesis.

2

Overhead falls

Kairos will benchmark every new build. Convergence towards Pearl's 1-5% target would change providers' economics.

3

Named providers

Established inference companies publicly mining, and more subsidised endpoints, would be the first real proof of demand.

4

Privacy in 2027

Native privacy would add Zcash's selling point to a network that already has post-quantum signatures.

5

Compute marketplace

A market for buying and hedging GPU time, settled in PEARL, would give the coin a use beyond saving.

6

Wider access

Coinbase and Kraken opened in November. Custody, further listings and, eventually, investment products would broaden the buyer base.

09 · Risks

What Could Go Wrong

A long list, stated plainly. Several are material.

Our approach to an asset like this starts with a more basic question than position size: should we hold it at all? For a seven-month-old network with a contested central claim, the answer turns on whether the potential reward justifies the risks below at today's price. If those dangers look too material, the better course is to monitor rather than invest, waiting for a more favourable balance of risk and reward as the evidence proves or disproves the thesis. Even its champions accept the downside: one of its most vocal backers says it is entirely reasonable to assume Pearl goes to zero.

1Useful work may never arrive

Under 1% of blocks show genuine AI activity, and the first FP8 test found mining costs 20-40% of a server's output against a 1-5% target. If providers do not adopt it within months of mainnet, the core thesis weakens to "a newer Bitcoin".

2Relentless dilution and selling

Supply grows around 75% in a year, and miners sell close to 90% of what they earn. Without strong new demand, the price falls even if nothing goes wrong.

3Insiders and transparency

The lab benefited from easy early mining, has not disclosed its holdings, and raised $130m of equity whose investors can profit even if the coin does not. The lock-up cannot be enforced onchain.

4Security on borrowed hardware

One analyst estimates around 200 MW of GPUs could take majority control. Unlike Bitcoin's single-use chips, an attacker's GPUs keep their value afterwards, lowering the cost of an attack.

5Upgrade execution

FP8 will remove about two-thirds of mining power, and earlier forks were fixed in public code only 3 to 24 hours before activation. An open bug lets miners find blocks the verifier rejects.

6Thin liquidity

Order books held about $410k of offers and $220k of bids within 2% of the price on 18 November. Even a mid-sized sale can shift the price; exit at size is not guaranteed.

7Narrative and promotion

Much of the bull case is advanced by holders. Sceptics argue every "alternative store of value" relies on finding the next buyer, and that a new contender each year weakens all of them.

8Single-supplier dependence

FP8 mining is limited to Nvidia's newest chips. Pearl's fortunes are tied to one company's product roadmap and to the pricing of AI compute.

10 · How Pearl fits the Dragonfly approach

A Monetary Bet, Sized as One

Pearl fails our cash-flow tests by design. That changes where it belongs, not whether it is interesting.

We test every liquid asset with the same questions an early-stage technology investor would ask: is the user base growing, does each newcomer make the network more valuable, is there real revenue, and is it returned to holders. Pearl's answers are unusual.

Our core holdings are applications that earn and share revenue, which now make up 61% of the fund. Pearl is not one of them, and we will not pretend otherwise. It belongs instead with Zcash in a small store-of-value sleeve: assets whose return comes from winning a share of the market for digital money rather than from cash flows. We hold Zcash as a privacy overlay; Pearl would play the equivalent role for the AI build-out.

Our discipline sets the terms. We value tokens on circulating market value, never fully diluted, which for Pearl means accepting that every month held brings dilution the price must outrun. High-risk positions are held at smaller-than-average size, with liquidity as our main risk lever; the Coinbase and Kraken listings make an orderly entry and exit possible for the first time, though order books remain thin. The fund is long-only with no leverage, so the perpetual futures market is irrelevant to us except as a source of volatility.

Our stance: Pearl goes on our active watch list rather than into the portfolio today. The potential reward is large, but at this stage the dangers set out above, from unproven useful work to heavy dilution and opaque insider holdings, are too material for a favourable balance of risk and reward. We would consider a small position, sized as high-risk and built gradually rather than chased after a 37% week, only once traction begins to prove out the thesis. Until then we will monitor the FP8 mainnet date and independent overhead benchmarks, the share of blocks doing real AI work, the number of named providers mining, miners' sell-through rate and exchange depth, and any disclosure of the lab's holdings, and revisit our view as the evidence demands.

Conclusion

Gold Without the Copper, For Now

Pearl has taken one of the oldest unsolved problems in cryptography, making proof-of-work useful, and turned it into a live network, listed on the largest exchanges, within seven months. Its premise is elegant: let the AI industry's own hardware secure a new store of value as a by-product, one that becomes safer as AI expands. If Bitcoin's security budget does become a problem, Pearl is the most credible answer anyone has shipped.

But by-product credits only exist when the main mine is running. Today Pearl's miners are almost all prospectors digging for the gold alone and selling it at the gate, while heavy issuance and opaque insider holdings weigh on the price. The FP8 upgrade will tell us, within months, whether real AI providers start running the copper mine.

That is the shape of an asymmetric bet: a plausible path to ten times or more, a real chance of losing most of the investment, and a clear set of milestones that will tell us which way it is going. We will watch closely, and size modestly only if traction and the balance of risk and reward start to prove out the thesis.

As long-term investors, it is immaterial, perhaps even advantageous, that this is not yet the consensus view.
